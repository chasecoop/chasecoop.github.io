title: "On AI"
pubDate: 2026-10-31
---

My first interaction with ML tooling was many years ago. We use a technique in rigging called Pose Space Deformation. This is a small form of an AI model. Basically, it lets you predict how a character would articulate based on the current pose. This would save time and money if you didn't have to do this on every shot or every character. At least that's the promise. My next AI interactions were with Midjourney when it launched. I stayed up for hours a few nights, so addicted to it.

I was entranced by Midjourney. I could create anything in my imagination and found it one of the most addicting processes I had ever experienced. To me, it never felt like it removed the artistic process, or at least the parts of it I liked. I love art because it lets you play with ideas and create a message visually, or not. Many times art turns into trash because you can't realize your vision. Midjourney enabled me to rapidly conjure my vision to life in a matter of minutes.

Enter 2026. Writing code is now gone. Every engineer I know doesn't write code, including me. Do I miss it? Not really, to be honest. Code was always a tool. It was a screwdriver that worked very efficiently for me, but now we've invented a drill and I want to use the drill for everything. I've had ideas for years and decades I've wanted to try, and now with AI I'm able to execute those ideas without sacrificing my job, friends, life around me. I've discovered some of those ideas were really good ideas, but many were not, and that's been a weird feeling.

Next on the docket for AI to take is 3D creation, my personal passion. A year ago it was incapable; today it can model the entirety of New York City in an hour or less to accurate detail, utilizing Blender or making its own 3D modeling tool. Just like that, 3D creation tools have been made obsolete. Similar to code, I'm able to try ideas I never had time for and focus directly on what I want to make vs. fighting the tools, which, let's be honest, we've all struggled with for years. No one uses these 3D apps out of the box. You spend years customizing them to your liking, in most cases just to make them work so you can go home at night. Those days are gone, and honestly, I don't think I'll miss it. What does this mean for my job, since I'm a 3D artist? I do not know, but I do know the 3rd and maybe 4th quarter of my career will be creating things with AI.

I will not write these blog posts with AI. These are my own words. I've only had AI help me spell-check them. I've found myself becoming exhausted from reading AI-generated documents. It's a rampant problem at work, and I'm not completely innocent. Lately I've been using AI to help me write, and sometimes completely rewriting the document. I appreciate human-built documents with errors, weird grammar, and just more human. It's a human story when written, but with AI it's a mechanical aftertaste. It's the difference of eating a steak cooked medium, or a McDonald's hamburger patty.
